# A2. Master dc1-s1 cut off with ICMP reject, 30s

Run: `experiments/runs/A2-20261020-165850`. Same as A1 but the router answers
every packet to/from dc1-s1 with ICMP admin-prohibited instead of dropping it.

## Cluster

Identical to A1: PFAIL at ~11s, FAIL and election at ~13s, dc3-s1 promoted
(epoch 24). After the heal at 35s dc1-s1 rejoined as a replica, link up at ~40s.

## Client

Identical to A1 during the incident: first `io:timed out` at 8.0s, 16
timeouts every 3s on shard 1, shards 2 and 3 starved by head-of-line blocking
(ok=0 two seconds out of three).

After the heal (35.0s) shard 1 kept timing out until the load ended at 51.8s.
Verification then timed out twice more; the slot refresh ran at 58.8s, 23.8s
after the heal, again triggered by the first request that reached dc1-s1 and
got MOVED.

240 errors, all `io:timed out`, 0 lost writes.

## What happened

ICMP unreachable is a *soft* error for an established TCP socket on Linux: the
kernel records it but does not abort the connection; the data stays queued and
is retransmitted with exponential backoff exactly as in the blackhole case.
So from redis-rs's point of view A2 and A1 are the same incident: timeouts,
no refresh, a stale socket that revives only when its retransmit timer fires
after the heal (here ~24s after 30s of outage, versus ~46s after 60s in A1).

A fast ICMP reject would only have helped a *new* connection (see I2), and
redis-rs never opens one here because a timeout never triggers a reconnect.

## Went well / wrong

Same as A1. Lost writes: 0.

## Implications for redis-rs

Same as A1. One extra point: a reconnect-on-timeout policy would have turned
this case into A3-like behaviour (the fresh connect fails instantly with
`EHOSTUNREACH`, the node is marked reconnecting, the request falls back to a
replica, MOVED arrives, the slot map is refreshed) and the outage would have
ended with the failover at ~13s.
