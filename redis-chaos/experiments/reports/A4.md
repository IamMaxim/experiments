# A4. SIGSTOP master dc3-s3 (hung process), 30s

Run: `experiments/runs/A4-20261020-165953`. `docker pause` freezes the process;
the kernel keeps the sockets open and ACKs incoming data.

## Cluster

PFAIL at ~12s, FAIL at ~17s, dc1-s3 promoted (epoch 26). After `unpause` at
35s dc3-s3 woke up believing it was master, saw the higher epoch, and became a
replica of dc1-s3 (link up ~41s). No lost writes.

## Client

Same shape as A1 during the incident: first `io:timed out` at 8.0s, then 16
timeouts every 3s on shard 3 only, 144 errors total, healthy shards starved by
the harness's head-of-line blocking.

The difference is the heal. `beginning slot refresh` at **35.0s**, the moment
the process resumed. The frozen node had ACKed every request into its socket
buffer, so on resume it processed the backlog, answered the first write with
MOVED (it had already learned it was demoted), and the client refreshed at
once. Max latency 2.5s, errors stopped within the same second.

## Reading

A hung process and a blackholed one look identical to redis-rs while they
last (timeouts, no refresh, stuck on the stale master until something answers
MOVED), but they recover very differently: the hang recovers instantly on
unpause because TCP is intact, the blackhole (A1/A2) waits for the retransmit
backoff.

The 27s between failover (~17s) and heal (35s) were avoidable in both cases;
the library had no mechanism to notice the new master.

## Implications for redis-rs

Same as A1: timeouts must count against the node; after a few of them the
client should refresh the topology from a different node.
